## Not confirmed in the articles checked

These are well-known claims from ancient or traditional sources, kept as written, but the Wikipedia articles consulted did not state them directly. Verify before publishing.

- Cineas: Pyrrhus's remark that he won more cities with words than Pyrrhus did with arms; the Senate as "an assembly of kings" (both from Plutarch).
- Ban Chao: the party of 36 men (Book of the Later Han).
- Lu Jia: the second mission to Nanyue and the "won on horseback" remark (Records of the Grand Historian).
- Arminius: "Varus, give me back my legions!" (Suetonius).
- Ptolemy: that his too-small Earth encouraged Columbus.
- Attila: eating from a wooden plate (Priscus).
- Al-Masudi: writing about the kings of the Franks.
- Clavijo: travelling alongside a giraffe; Timur's failing sight.
- Frederick II: the travelling menagerie.
- Marco Polo: Columbus's annotated copy.
- Washington: George III's "greatest man in the world" remark; "lost more battles than he won".
- Bolívar: orphaned young.
- Toussaint Louverture: worked as a coachman.
- Mongol crisis: Novgorod as the example of a state that submitted.
- Leonardo: about 30 dissections; hardening of the arteries.
- Li Shizhen: died before the book was printed.
